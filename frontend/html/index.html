The loop

Report, prioritise, fund, verify.

Four moves. Three of them are transactions anyone can audit on Etherscan.

  1. 01

    A resident reports it

    Photo, description and an estimated cost, filed against their sector. It sits as a draft until the ward opens a voting round.

    Draft
  2. 02

    The neighbourhood prioritises

    Everyone gets 100 credits per round. Votes cost the square of your total, so nobody can quietly buy the outcome with volume.

    Voting
  3. 03

    The budget goes into escrow

    The winning problem is funded. The contractor receives half immediately; the Treasury contract holds the rest.

    In progress
  4. 04

    Residents settle it

    The contractor marks the job done and a 3-day vote opens. Approve and they're paid. Reject and the money returns to the treasury.

    Completed

Why it's different

Most civic apps ask you to trust a dashboard.

A dashboard is a claim. It shows you a number somebody typed. The interesting question is whether the money could have moved without you — and on a database, it always could.

Here the second half of every budget lives in Treasury.sol with no function that releases it early. Not for the contractor, not for the ward officer, not for whoever deployed the contract. finalize() reads the vote result and refuses to run while the vote is still open.

Quadratic voting

Intensity without capture.

One-person-one-vote can't tell a mild preference from an urgent one. Unlimited voting just hands the outcome to whoever shows up most.

Squaring the cost lets someone whose street floods every monsoon spend hard on that one problem — while making it prohibitively expensive to dominate every problem at once. The price is shown before you commit, and credits only refill when the ward opens a new round.

Three seats

Everyone sees the same ledger.

Resident

Report problems, spend credits to prioritise, and cast the vote that unlocks or returns the remaining budget.

Contractor

See the jobs funded to your wallet, post progress updates, and open the verification vote when the work is finished.

Ward office

Open voting rounds, fund the winning problem into escrow, and settle it once residents have returned a verdict.
